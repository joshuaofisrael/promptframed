Poster guide

How to turn ChatGPT art into a framed poster

You already have the picture. The rest is a file that can survive a wall, a page people can open, and a print shop that mails the paper. Prompt Framed does this for the Night Windows gallery. The same path works for any single image you are willing to stand behind.

Make a picture that still works in a room

Phone screens forgive a lot. A poster does not. Before you export, look at the image from across the room in your mind. The subject should read at a glance. Tiny captions, watermarks, and edge-to-edge detail get cut or look cramped once the paper has a margin.

Portrait 2:3 is a natural poster shape. Moonlit Alpine Meadow is 1024 by 1536 pixels, which is that ratio. It is a strong file for a smaller print. It is a soft file for a very large one. At about 150 dots per inch, 1024 by 1536 covers roughly 7 by 10 inches. Bigger paper stretches those same pixels. If you want a large poster, regenerate or upscale to a higher-resolution file before you send it to the shop.

Night scenes print darker than they look on a bright phone. If the sky is nearly black, lift the shadows a little so the ridge and the flowers still separate on paper. Keep the warm windows. They are what the eye finds first.

Export a clean file

Save a PNG of the full picture and keep that file as the print master. Make a smaller JPEG or WebP for the gallery page so the site stays fast. Do not upload a screenshot of a chat window. Crop away the interface, and do not leave a prompt sitting in the corner of the art.

Check three things before you call the file done:

  • The important shapes sit inside the image, not on the outer edge that a frame can cover.
  • Faces, type, and windows are not warped.
  • You know the pixel size, so you can pick a poster size that matches it.

Sell the poster first, then the frame

Start with one product: a poster. People understand it, it ships flat or rolled, and it is the sentence in the caption. A framed print is the second product for the same picture, with its own shop link, once you know the frame style and size you actually want to offer.

Print on demand means you do not buy a stack of posters in advance. When someone orders, the shop prints that one and mails it to them. Prompt Framed is set up for that with Printful. The gallery page holds the button. The shop holds the payment. Until the shop link is real, the button stays on the page and does not pretend to charge anyone.

Give the picture its own page

The buy link in a caption should open a page about that picture, not a homepage full of other things and not a dead end. On the page, show the image large, say what it is in a sentence or two, and put Buy Poster where a thumb can reach it. Keep a second control for the framed print so the two products stay distinct.

That page is also the thing you can share twice: once from Instagram, and again when someone asks “where do I get it?” The address does not change when you later connect the shop. Only the button’s destination changes.

Write the caption so the link is the last thing

Instagram is the front door. The caption does not need a speech. Name the picture, give one line of mood, then the buy line. This is the pattern on every Prompt Framed piece:

Want a poster? Buy at the link: https://joshuaofisrael.github.io/promptframed/art/moonlit-alpine-meadow/

The link is the piece page. The piece page has the button. When the print shop is connected, that button opens the product. People who tap the caption land on the picture they just saw, which is the moment they decide.

Say what the picture is

If a picture may be made with ChatGPT or another image tool, say so where a visitor can find it without turning the gallery into a technical note. Prompt Framed keeps that sentence in the footer and on the About page. The pictures themselves are titled as pictures.

A worked example

Moonlit Alpine Meadow is Photo 1. The print master lives with the gallery files. The page shows a lighter copy, a short description, Buy Poster, Framed Print, and a caption you can copy. The caption ends with the buy link. Checkout waits until a real Printful URL is saved. Nothing on the page invents a price or takes a card.
